## 9. Why the slice stopped at one cell — and what the owner ordered next

The owner's ruling: paging changes what is resident, not how many steps a task needs, so the
paged arm would walk the same 150 steps into the same wall, and the both-arms rule would then
declare a fixture defect. The $0.33 and 40 minutes would confirm arithmetic. Chunk 1 closes at
one cell. Ordered next, in this order:

1. **Restart the gateway onto 0.10.4 before any further paid cell** — not mid-chunk, not "next
   time": every grid cell must run on one instrument, or a pair compares a capture that can
   prove its arm with one that cannot.
2. **Re-scope T1 by steps, not tokens.** The lever is calls: 20 files read whole at ~2.2k tokens
   each keeps the ≥45k floor at roughly 25–40 calls. Thirty-three 12 KB parts is 33 calls of
   pure reading before any measurement begins.
3. **Fix the metric before the slice.** With no `turn.render`, turn-level NTR is underivable
   from a `-z` capture. Two honest options, one to be chosen and preregistered: call `render()`
   from `on_pre_api_request` (making one "turn" equal one API request — a semantic change the
   design must own), or define NTR on the `perApiRequest` series.
4. **Settle the `maxLineBytes` definition** (the 4–9 byte question) offline against fixture
   bytes before the next paid call, or the first passing arm will fail on an undefined rule.

Deadline recorded by the owner: a green slice by end of tomorrow, or W8 goes on record as
BLOCKED with six harness defects and this measured cost per cell.
