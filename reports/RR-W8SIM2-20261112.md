# RR-W8SIM2-20261112 — scoring the simulator: it fails, and the failure names the missing record

Zero spend. Input: the cell-03 pair's two captures (control `local-87612ee9b0…`, treatment
`local-c0b4ff6295…`), budget 60,000. Tool: `tools/w8-sim-score.py`.

## The rule, declared in the tool before the numbers were read


## What the failure says

The residual is **systematic, not noisy**: it grows about **3,200 tokens per turn** and changes sign
once, at the onset. That shape is a granularity artefact, not a policy error. A turn in this harness is
**two model requests**; the policy decides per request, and the capture records one `turn.render` per
turn with one `evictedTokens`. So the model compares one decision against the sum of two, and the second
call of each turn carries roughly 3.2 k more than the first — which is exactly the residual's slope.

The onset is the same artefact seen once: at turn 11 the control's first request is 62,185, above the
budget, but the engine evicted nothing, because what the engine sees at that moment still fits once the
protected set and the unrecorded scaffold are subtracted.

**A measurement note that cost one iteration and is worth keeping:** `call.meta.providerUsage` is *not*
the prompt series. Summing `inputTokens + cachedInputTokens` gave 68 k at turn 6 and 204 k at turn 19 —
roughly twice the request the policy saw — because it aggregates the turn's calls. The counterfactual
must be `turn.render.prefix.approxInputTokens`, and the first version of the loader used the wrong one
and produced a 3.5× ratio.

## What was built in response (plugin 0.13.3)

`select_context` now reports `beforeTokens` and `afterTokens` for **each selection**, and the plugin
records them per session in the sidecar (`captures.callSizes`, capped at 80 entries). With that field a
simulator can be scored at the granularity the policy decides at, instead of one turn against two calls.

That is not a result yet: no capture in the tree carries the field, so the next scoring run needs one
pair written by 0.13.3 — free tier, $0.00 — and the same pre-declared rule, restated at call
granularity before it is read.

## What may be claimed

Supportable: "at turn granularity, a keep-law simulator over the measured control series predicts the
paging onset one turn early and under-predicts total evicted tokens by 31 % (190,571 against 275,362);
the residual's slope, ~3.2 k per turn, matches the second call's growth, which the trace does not
record separately."

Not supportable: that the policy is wrong (the engine's own per-turn counts are measured), or that the
simulator is useless (it is unscoreable at this granularity, not disproven).
