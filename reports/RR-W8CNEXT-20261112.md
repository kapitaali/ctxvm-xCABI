## 3. The cache-miss multiplier, measured — this is the P8 question with a number

From the same run, via the hermes DB's own usage counters:

| arm | input_tokens (uncached) | cache_read |
|---|---|---|
| naive | 224,455 | 4,461,568 |
| paged | **1,071,090** (+846,635) | 2,870,144 (−0.64×) |

The paged arm's extra **uncached** input is **846,635 tokens** — that is **4.57× the 185,303 tokens it
evicted**. The mechanism is the one the cache-stability note predicted: a rewritten prefix misses the
cache, so the whole prefix re-sends on the next turn, and it keeps missing while the selection keeps
changing. **Read as an observation, not a law**: at this shape, one non-cache-stable selection costs
several times more in re-sent prefix than it saves in the request. That is the number the next card
needs before it proposes any "paging saves tokens" framing — and it is exactly the reviewer packet's
question (P8, cache-stable selection), now with a measured ratio ✓.

**And the arithmetic that follows, for the owner:** on a paid model, a policy that is not cache-stable
does not merely fail to save — it *spends*. Any future claim about savings must therefore be a claim
about **cache-stable** selection, and the break-even formula the books already carry
(n\* = (w/r)(P′/S)) has to be re-derived for a policy that rewrites the prefix every turn, because S
(the saving per call) is now bounded by the *request* saving while P′ (the cache-write cost) is not
small. **No saving may be claimed from any of this** (law 7) ✓.
