# RR-W8GRID-20261110 — C7: grid feasibility at the measured call counts

Zero spend. Rates: cell-01's own cost ($0.1827 / 29 calls = **$0.0063/call**) and the provider's
blended day rate ($7.0519 / 819 = **$0.00861/call**).

## 1. The grid at 29 calls per cell

```
pairs   calls (2 arms)   at $0.0063/call   at $0.00861/call
  48        2,784              $17.54            $23.97
  24        1,392               $8.77            $11.99
  12          696               $4.38             $5.99
   6          348               $2.19             $3.00
```

The earlier projections assumed 13–20 calls per cell. At the measured 29, a 48-pair grid consumes
**$17.5–$24.0** — all of the remaining cap, or more of it than exists, since ≥ $9 is already spent and
the exact figure is UNKNOWN (C5).

## 2. The call-count asymmetry poisons the ratio

In the one measured pair the control took 5 calls and the treatment 14. NTR is computed from the
receipt (`1 − paged_input/naive_input`), and input is the sum over calls — so a treatment that takes
three times as many calls pays three times the input **before any policy effect**. At 29 calls per
session the asymmetry is structural: prompts that produce tool calls cost more receipts regardless of
what the engine does. Any NTR from paired cells conflates route length with policy.

## 3. Detectable effect, with zero observed failures

Rule of three: the 95% upper bound on a failure rate with zero failures in n pairs is ≈ 3/n.

```
n = 4   75.0%
n = 6   50.0%
n = 8   37.5%
n = 12  25.0%
n = 30  10.0%
```

The preregistered non-inferiority gate (10 points) needs about **30 pairs with zero failures**, or a
binomially-designed interval if failures occur. A 6-pair grid cannot support it, and neither can 12.

## 4. Restated gate, proposed

The cells the cap can buy support a **descriptive** claim, not a non-inferiority one:

- supportable: "on this task and surface, at budget B, the paged arm produced a parsed-equal artefact
  in n of n cells; evictions were x; the receipt was y× the control" — a measurement with n stated.
- not supportable: any non-inferiority or equivalence statement, any cost saving, any claim that
  generalises beyond this task, model and host.
- not supportable for a different reason (C3): a saving larger than 5.3% of session input tokens,
  because the keep law leaves nothing unprotected for the first nine turns.

## 5. Recommendation to the owner (this card proposes; the owner decides)

A 48-pair grid is not affordable and could not pass its own gate. If a mechanism test is wanted at
all, 4–6 pairs at a budget that actually evicts (below ~66,600) buys a descriptive statement about
eviction and artefact equality — not about savings. Otherwise the negative result is already written.
