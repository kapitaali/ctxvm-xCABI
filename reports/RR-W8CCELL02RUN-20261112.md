## 2. The unit lesson, and this lane's own onset error, explained

Their row records the deviation honestly and draws the right rule: *"volume 210,856 vs the predicted
5-20 k — my specification error, `evictedTokens` sums per-turn re-drops rather than unique content; the
review lane's pre-run estimate (~185 k) was within 12 % on magnitude and wrong on onset (turn 4) …
**Rule recorded: a block's volume column must name its unit.**"* ✓

**Both halves of that are right, and the arithmetic explains them:**

- **Two different volumes.** The trace's counter sums **per-turn re-drops** (a page dropped and later
  re-dropped counts again); this lane's ~185 k was the **residency difference** — the *net* request
  reduction. They are different quantities, 12 % apart ✓ — so the review-side estimate was right in
  magnitude and measured the wrong thing to be a prediction ✓.
- **The onset is not a turn number.** This lane's "turn 4" was wrong because **the onset is the first turn
  the *unconstrained* request exceeds the budget** — at turn 4 the request was ~30 k against a 60 k
  budget, so nothing was over budget ✓. The control crossed 60,000 at turn 11 (62,999) and the eviction
  began at turn 13 ⇒ the **engine's lag is two turns here** ✓.

**So the next block's two columns should be re-expressed in these units** (their rule, applied):

```
onset    the first turn the unconstrained request exceeds the budget, plus the engine's lag
         (2 turns measured) — NOT a turn number
volume   the NET request reduction (sum of unconstrained minus budget), named as such; it is a
         different number from the trace's per-turn re-drop sum, and the verifier should compare
         against the quantity the block names
```

And the onset is *predictable before the run* from the control's series ✓ — which is the useful part: a
block can now say "the first turn the request exceeds the budget" and be falsifiable ✓.
