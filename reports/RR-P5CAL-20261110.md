## The verdicts as recorded

**Verdict 1 — publish-with-caveat now; wait for a larger held-out corpus before any transfer
claim.** Calibration stays fitted-on-umpire-corpus and ships as exactly that, with the measured
figures and the explicit statement that transfer to other label sets is UNESTABLISHED. "No
detectable degradation" is not "no degradation" — the CIs including zero mean the data cannot
tell; the point estimates DID go the wrong way (A1 +4.7 points, A2 +1.9), and A1's point
estimate already EXCEEDS the 2-point effect the design wanted excluded. Shipping a per-(pin,
seed,label) TRANSFER guarantee would be unsupported by the current evidence — not shipped.

**Verdict 2 — what would close it.** A preregistered equivalence test on a held-out corpus with
SEVERAL new label sets (the generalisation unit is the label SET, not the row; fewer than about
five would not convince), cluster bootstrap across sets; margin ±2 points; Brier or log-loss
primary, ECE secondary (small-sample bin-occupancy bias), cross-fitting for calibration. Corpus
sizing rule: from the existing rows paired bootstrap half-width h, n ≈ 180 × (h/2)²  (h=4 →
~720; h=6 → ~1,620). Per-scope fitting needs per-scope rows — isotonic over a few dozen rows
per scope will overfit; report rows per scope. All parameters preregistered BEFORE the run.

**Verdict 3 — the caller sentence (PROPOSAL, verbatim from the reviewer):**

> Calibrated confidence was fitted on umpire's own label sets and has not been shown to
> transfer, so treat it as an uncalibrated score for your labels until you check it on a sample
> of your own labelled rows.

**Falsifier** (registered, both directions): a held-out set with new label sets where calibrated
ECE exceeds raw ECE by more than the margin ⇒ do not ship the transfer claim at all; if the
equivalence test passes inside the margin ⇒ ship it.
