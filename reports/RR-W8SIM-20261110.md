# RR-W8SIM-20261110 — C3: offline budget simulation, and where it stops

Zero spend. Input: `w8-cell-01` (12 prompts, 29 requests, budget 55,000, 0 evictions). Rule written
before the numbers: the simulation is valid only if it reproduces the observed outcome at 55,000
(**zero** evictions) and the measured floor of 53,790; otherwise stop and say why.

## 1. It does not validate, and that is the result

Two models were tried.

**Model A — pages only.** Requests are rebuilt from declared page tokens (4,498 / 4,415 / … / 8,926)
plus the system prompt (21,932 B ≈ 5,483). At 55,000 it predicts **12,308 tokens evicted over two
turns**; observed: **0**.

**Model B — measured request minus the middle.** Uses the measured `approxInputTokens` series
(10,771 → 74,751) and subtracts the pages that the keep law leaves unprotected. At 55,000 it predicts
**22,558 evicted over three turns**; observed: **0**.

Both fail the same way: `_plan` subtracts **scaffold** — messages that are neither hard-kept nor pages,
i.e. the assistant text carrying tool calls — and the capture does not record it. With the page-only
floor at 53,790 against a 55,000 budget, only 1,210 tokens separate the prediction from the observation,
which is exactly the unmeasured scaffold.

**So: the offline simulator cannot be validated from this capture, because the capture stores pages and
request totals but not the message-level mass the policy subtracts.** Per the card's rule, the
simulation stops here.

## 2. What can still be said without a simulator — the structural ceiling

The keep law protects the system prompt, the first three turns and the last six. On a 12-turn session
that leaves a middle only from turn 9 onward:

```
turn  middle turns  middle tokens  request   share of request
  8        0                0       51,445        0.0%
  9        1            4,209       56,363        7.5%
 10        2            8,368       66,070       12.7%
 11        3           12,827       74,751       17.2%

cumulative ceiling over the session    25,404 of 479,076 request tokens   = 5.3%
```

**No policy can save more than 5.3% of the session's input tokens on this shape**, because for the
first nine turns there is nothing unprotected to evict. The reviewer's 12% figure is the same quantity
measured at the last request; the 5.3% is the session-weighted one.

Eviction also needs the budget **below floor + middle ≈ 66,600**; at 72,000 the middle (12,827) fits
inside the headroom, so the planned cell would evict **zero**, as the reviewer predicted.

## 3. The budget question, restated correctly

The earlier reasoning — "72,000 sits in the window" — compared the budget against the naive
end-of-run resident (61,201) and the peak request (79,960). The quantity that decides eviction is
`budget − floor − scaffold − pinned` versus the middle. With floor ≈ 53,790 and middle ≤ 12,827, any
budget above ~66,600 evicts nothing, and any budget below it evicts at most that middle.

**A budget cannot be chosen to make this task informative.** The shape caps the effect at 5.3%.

## 4. What the simulator needs to run (proposal, not applied)

```
record scaffoldTokens per request in the coverage sidecar (perApiRequest already exists;
one more field), or record the message list sizes, not just page sizes
```

With that field the `_plan` arithmetic is reproducible offline and a budget sweep becomes a real
measurement instead of an estimate.
